How the system works, and how it gets built

Two things people want before they commit: what the equipment on their roof actually does, and what the four weeks between a phone call and a running system involve. Both are below.

Updated September 2026
The systemHow it gets builtQuestions

Follow the power

Select any part of the system to see what it does and how it is specified.

Sunlight → your appliances → storage → grid

Panels

N-type monocrystalline panels convert daylight to DC electricity. Layout is set by roof orientation, tilt and shading, with panels grouped into strings that behave the same way through the day.

500–550 W per panel25 yr product warranty30 yr performance warranty

Inverter

Converts DC from the roof into 230 V AC for the house, and decides in real time whether power goes to your appliances, the battery or the grid. Hybrid units handle both solar and battery.

97–98.5% peak efficiencyString, hybrid or micro10 yr warranty

Switchboard

Your switchboard is where the system ties into the house. It usually needs a dedicated solar circuit, isolators and, on older boards, an upgrade before the system can be connected.

Dedicated solar circuitIsolation both sidesChecked at assessment

Battery

Lithium iron phosphate storage for the solar you do not use during the day. It charges once the house is covered and discharges through the evening peak. A backup circuit can keep nominated loads running.

LiFePO4 chemistry10 yr warrantyBackup circuit optional

Your home

Appliances draw from solar first, then the battery, then the grid. Shifting heavy loads such as hot water, pool pumps and dishwashers into daylight hours is the cheapest way to lift self-consumption.

Solar first, then batteryNo changeover neededLoad shifting recommended

Grid

Anything left after the house and battery are covered is exported through your meter and credited at your feed-in rate. Your network sets an export limit, so the system is configured to stay inside it.

Export limit appliedFeed-in credited by retailerMeter reconfigured at connection

Monitoring

Generation, consumption, battery state and grid flow are logged and sent to an app. Microinverter and optimiser systems report per panel, so a shaded or failing panel is visible rather than guessed at.

5–15 min updatesPush and email alertsiOS, Android, web

Equipment we install: Trina Solar and AIKO panels, Sungrow and FoxESS inverters and batteries. Which model goes on your roof depends on the design, and the full datasheet for every component is attached to your quote.

How it gets built

Seven stages, about four weeks. Select a stage to see what happens and what is needed from you.

01

First contact

15 minutes, by phone or email

You send through your details and a recent bill. We check your address against our service area, look at your usage pattern and tell you whether solar, a battery, or both are worth quoting before anyone visits.

What we ask for

  • A recent electricity bill, ideally a full quarter
  • Your address and roof type
  • Whether you are considering a battery
  • Any planned changes: EV, pool, air conditioning

What you get

  • A straight answer on whether it is worth proceeding
  • A booking time for the site assessment
  • No obligation and nothing ordered
02

Site assessment

45 to 60 minutes, on site

We measure the roof, check how it is built and look at everything the system has to connect into. This is the step that decides whether a design is realistic, so it is done in person rather than from satellite imagery.

On the roof

  • Usable roof area, pitch and orientation
  • Rafter spacing and roof condition
  • Shading from trees, chimneys and neighbours
  • Cable route from array to inverter

Inside

  • Switchboard condition and spare capacity
  • Meter type and main supply
  • Where the inverter and battery can be mounted
  • Photos and measurements for the design file
03

Design and simulation

2 to 3 days

Your usage is modelled against simulated output for your specific roof, in 15-minute intervals across a full year. That produces the panel count, inverter size and battery size, and shows how much of your own generation you would actually use.

What gets modelled

  • Yearly generation for your roof pitch and orientation
  • Your consumption profile from the bill and interval data
  • Self-consumption with and without a battery
  • Export against your network limit

What you get

  • A fixed price quote, itemised
  • Panel layout on your roof
  • Expected generation and bill impact
  • The reasoning behind every number
04

Approvals and paperwork

1 to 2 weeks, handled by us

Before anything is installed the system has to be approved for connection and the rebate paperwork has to be set up. We lodge it, track it and tell you when it clears. Nothing is ordered until you have accepted the quote.

We handle

  • Network connection application
  • Rebate documentation and assignment
  • Equipment ordering once approved
  • Booking your install date

We need from you

  • Signed acceptance of the quote
  • Account holder details for the connection
  • Meter number from your bill
  • Access arrangements for install day
05

Installation day

Usually one day

A crew arrives in the morning, isolates the supply, mounts the racking and panels, runs the DC and AC cabling, installs the inverter and battery, and ties into your switchboard. Power is off for part of the day while the switchboard work is done.

On the day

  • Mounting rail and panel installation
  • DC cabling and isolators
  • Inverter and battery mounting
  • Switchboard connection and labelling

What to expect

  • An arrival window given the day before
  • A short planned power outage
  • Site cleaned and packaging removed
  • A walk-through before the crew leaves
06

Commissioning and handover

Same day

The system is tested end to end, the inverter is configured to your network settings, and your monitoring is set up on your phone before anyone leaves. We show you what normal looks like so an abnormal day is obvious later.

Testing

  • Full system test and fault checks
  • Inverter settings applied for your network
  • Battery charge and discharge verified
  • Backup circuit tested if fitted

Handover pack

  • Monitoring app set up and explained
  • Warranty documents for every component
  • System diagram and switchboard labels
  • Meter reconfiguration lodged with your retailer
07

After the install

Ongoing

Monitoring keeps running after handover. If output drops away from what the design predicted, that shows up in the data, and we would rather find it than have you notice it on a bill three months later.

Support

  • Monitoring alerts reviewed when raised
  • Warranty claims handled with the manufacturer
  • Help reading your first post-solar bill
  • Advice on shifting loads into daylight

If something is wrong

  • Contact us through the support form
  • Fault history pulled from monitoring
  • On-site visit if it cannot be resolved remotely
  • Written outcome for any warranty claim

Technical questions

Common questions about performance, blackouts, export and warranty.

Modern Tier 1 panels lose around 1% of output in the first year, then roughly 0.35–0.4% a year after that. Most are still producing 87% or more of their original output at 30 years, which is what the performance warranty guarantees.

Without a battery configured for backup, a grid-tied system shuts down during an outage as a safety requirement, even in full sun — so it cannot feed power into lines crews may be working on. A battery with a backup circuit keeps nominated loads running.

Yes. Your distribution network service provider sets an export limit, commonly around 5kW on a standard single-phase residential connection, though it varies by network and can sometimes be raised on application. It caps what you can sell back, not what you can generate and use yourself.

Three separate things: the product warranty on the hardware itself, the performance warranty on output over time, and our workmanship warranty on the installation. They run on different timeframes and we set all three out in writing before install.
