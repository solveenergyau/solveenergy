String or microinverter, matched to your roof

Process

How an install actually runs

No step here is skippable, and nothing is subcontracted out to someone who's never seen your file before.

01

Site assessment

A qualified assessor checks roof structure and orientation, shading across the day and across seasons, and your switchboard's capacity for the system size being considered.

02

Engineering & design

Panel layout, string configuration, and inverter sizing are calculated from your actual roof measurements and usage data, not a generic template. You see the design before anything is ordered.

03

Compliance & approvals

Installation follows AS/NZS 5033 and AS/NZS 4777, by CEC-accredited installers. Grid-connection approval through your local distribution network service provider is handled as part of the job, not billed as an extra.

04

Installation

Typically completed in a single day for residential systems. Roof penetrations are flashed and sealed to manufacturer specification, and all DC/AC wiring is isolated and tested before commissioning.

05

Commissioning & handover

Full system test, monitoring app set up on your phone, and a walkthrough of what the numbers mean. You get the compliance certificate and warranty documentation, not just a working system.

Technical FAQ

The questions people ask once they've done some reading

How much do panels actually degrade over 30 years?

Modern Tier 1 monocrystalline panels typically lose around 1% of output in the first year, then roughly 0.35–0.4% per year after that, meaning most panels still produce 87% or more of their original output after 30 years, backed by the manufacturer's performance warranty. The bulk of that loss happens gradually and linearly, not in a sudden drop-off.

What happens to my system during a blackout?

Without a battery with backup capability, a standard grid-tied system shuts off during a blackout as a safety requirement, even if the sun's out, so it doesn't feed power back into lines that repair crews may be working on. A battery with a backup circuit keeps essential loads running regardless.

Is there a limit to how much power I can export to the grid?

Yes. Your local distribution network service provider sets an export limit, commonly around 5kW for a standard single-phase residential connection, though this varies by network and can sometimes be increased on application. This affects how much of your solar you can sell back, not how much you can generate and use yourself.

What's actually covered under warranty?

Three separate warranties apply: a product warranty (defects in the panel or inverter itself), a performance warranty (guaranteed minimum output over time), and a workmanship warranty from us covering the installation itself. They run on different timeframes, and we'll set out all three in writing before installation.
